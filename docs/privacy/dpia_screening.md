# Impact-assessment screening (outline)

Status: outline for counsel (decision L2.3). It describes the agent as handed to Omega IFS in November 2026. Art. 35(1) requires an impact assessment where processing is likely to result in a high risk. The EDPB-endorsed WP29 guidelines (WP248 rev.01) give nine criteria for that. The Garante publishes its own list of processing that always needs one, in provvedimento n. 467 of 11 October 2018 (GU n. 269 of 19 November 2018; doc. web 9058979). Its 12 types are pre-screened below, for counsel to check.

## The scale the code allows

At the default settings (`config.py`):

- Each sending mailbox may send 5 emails on its first sending day and 3 more on each sending day after, up to 100 a day from its 33rd sending day (warm-up, on by default; `modules/warmup.py`). Every email to a contact counts against it: first emails, follow-ups, retries, and an operator's Direct Send, which goes through the main mailbox.
- The agent sends on weekdays from 8:00 to 19:00, at most 3 emails a day to one company domain, and, from one running agent, at most 30 an hour. An operator's Direct Send may go outside those hours.
- A person emailed who never replies is kept 365 days after the last email, or longer while a follow-up or a retry is still pending, at most by the longest follow-up delay (4 days by default); one who replied or became a lead, 730 days after the last exchange; one never emailed, 30 days after collection.

So, once warmed up:

| Sending mailboxes | Emails a day, at most | People emailed in a year, at most |
| ----------------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| 1                 | 100                   | 26,100                            |
| 2                 | 200                   | 52,200                            |
| 3                 | 300                   | 78,300                            |
| 5                 | 500                   | 130,500                           |

- A year has about 261 weekdays. In a mailbox's first year the ramp makes its figure about 24,500.
- The pace of 30 an hour gives one running agent at most 330 emails a day in the send window, so from four mailboxes up the pace, not the allowance, limits that agent's own sends: 330 a day, or about 86,000 a year.
- Because a person emailed without a reply is kept 365 days, the last column also caps how many such people are held at one time, apart from those with a follow-up or a retry still pending. To it add those who replied or became leads in the past two years, and the contacts collected in the last 30 days and not emailed. Erased people stay as tokens, with no end set by the agent (L5.2).
- Nothing caps the number of mailboxes: the settings take four besides the main one, and `SENDER_ACCOUNTS` takes any number. The settings can also raise both limits: `WARMUP_MAX_DAILY` has no upper bound, and `EMAILS_PER_HOUR` goes up to 100. With warm-up off there is no daily allowance at all, and only the pace and the send window limit the day.
- Collection has no cap of its own. With warm-up on, the continuous loop stops collecting while as many contacts wait as the mailboxes can email within 30 days at today's allowance (L1.8); with no search queued, it also stops looking for new searches at 300 waiting contacts (`MAX_PENDING_BACKLOG`). Contacts screened out (clients, competitors, those judged irrelevant) stop counting as waiting once screened, and each is erased 30 days after collection (L1.13).

## Recommendation

At least two criteria apply: the scoring of people, and the combining of data. On the WP29 rule of thumb that two criteria usually call for an assessment, the developer recommends a full assessment before the pilot. On the Garante's list, the developer reads the facts as touching types 1 (if the scale is large), 7 (if AI is used) and 9. Counsel decides.

## What the full assessment will cover

- The data flows (`record_of_processing.md`).
- The risks to the people contacted: unwanted email, ranking by job title, inaccurate or out-of-date data, and the loss or copying of the database, its backups or the journal (`breach_procedure.md`).
- How the agent reads websites, and its robots.txt check (`lia_template.md`; L1.11).
- The safeguards of the build plan's steps G1 to G20, all built, listed with their status at the end of `decisions_for_counsel.md`, and what each leaves to counsel.
- The residual risk, and counsel's sign-off.
