# Record of processing: B2B email outreach (outline)

Status: outline for counsel, completed by hand from the finished product before the pilot (decision L2.2). GDPR Art. 30(1) lists what the record must contain; each heading below is one of its letters. Facts describe the agent as handed to Omega IFS in November 2026; what is not built names its step or the decision it waits on. Open points name the question in `decisions_for_counsel.md` that settles them.

## (a) Controller

- Omega IFS, with the legal entity name, registered office and registration number set under decision L6.
- Privacy contact: the address chosen under L2.6.
- Data protection officer: none, unless counsel decides otherwise under L2.6.

## (b) Purpose

- Finding European manufacturers that may buy small metal parts made to their drawings, and offering them Omega IFS's cold-formed fasteners and precision parts by email.
- Recognising and honouring objections, opt-outs and data-subject requests.
- Passing interested replies to Omega's sales staff.

## (c) People and data

**People:** employees and representatives of prospective business customers. These are the data subjects. The companies themselves are not data subjects under the GDPR, which protects natural persons (Art. 4(1)). They are protected as subscribers by art. 130 Codice Privacy (L1.2).

The code also contains routines that write contacts and emails to CSV files; no command or dashboard page calls them.

No special categories of data are sought.

## (d) Recipients

- Microsoft, for the sending mailbox (processor).
- The provider that first receives mail sent to Omega's domain (not yet checked for Omega; F-20 was found on an earlier placeholder domain), unless decision I1 moves that mail to Microsoft 365 (processor, L2.5).
- An address-verification service, if adopted (processor, L2.5).
- No outside AI provider. The AI runs only on a model on Omega's own server (`LLM_PROVIDER=openai_compatible`, `LLM_BASE_URL`, `LLM_MODEL`, for example Ollama), and the address must be on this machine or Omega's private network, so no outside AI company receives data. Whether AI may be used at all is still for counsel (L2.4).
- The host that serves `BASE_URL`, and a tunnel provider if one is used (`docs/DEPLOY.md`, section 3, suggests cloudflared or ngrok for testing). Every click on an unsubscribe link, and every one-click request, reaches it with the IP address it came from, and so does any use of the dashboard through that address (L2.7).
- The developer, and any hosting provider, if they have access to the production data (processors, L2.7).
- Omega's sales staff who receive lead alerts, by email today; in Teams only as decided under L5.11.

## (e) Transfers outside the EEA

- None planned beyond Microsoft's own, which rely on its data processing terms and standard contractual clauses [S141]. Microsoft 365's data location depends on the tenant's sign-up region (decision I5), and so does whether Microsoft's EU Data Boundary applies [S142]; Microsoft lists the transfers that continue under it [S143].
- No transfer for the AI: it runs only on Omega IFS's own server, so `transfer_assessment_llm.md` is kept for the record only (L2.4). Nothing reaches it unless `LLM_PROVIDER=openai_compatible` with `LLM_BASE_URL` and `LLM_MODEL`. With that server set, each use that sees a recipient's data needs its own switch, off by default except the company-country question (`COMPANY_COUNTRY_AI`), which sends no more than the fit check, and `AI_DPA_REFERENCE`, the reference of the provider's processing agreement (steps G10, G17). Every prompt is first stripped of addresses, phone numbers and the query part of web addresses. Search topics, which name no one, can go with a key alone.

## (f) Time limits for erasure

The periods below are the developer's proposals, built in step G12; each is pending counsel (L5.1). The agent applies them at its daily live run, and `main.py retention` applies them without the agent, for a daily scheduled task. Either erases only while `RETENTION_ENABLED` is on, as it is by default.

| Data                                                                                 | Proposed period, pending L5.1                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A contact never emailed                                                              | Erased 30 days after collection; a first email after that is refused (L1.8)                             |
| A contact emailed who never replied                                                  | Erased 365 days after the last email sent; not while a follow-up or a retry is pending, for at most the longest follow-up delay more |
| A contact who replied, or whose reply led to a lead alert                            | Erased 730 days after the last exchange: the last email, reply or lead alert                            |
| A contact who objected, declined or made a request by reply                          | Erased at the next daily run (L1.16)                                                                    |
| A contact at a client company                                                        | No exception: the same periods                                                                          |
| The subject and text of each email                                                   | As long as the contact; then "[erased]"                                                                 |
| The basis record and the unsubscribe-link records                                    | Deleted when the contact is erased (L5.9)                                                               |
| The reply text and recipients of a lead alert                                        | Cleared after 30 days                                                                                   |
| Addresses in the activity log                                                        | Masked to their first letter and domain after 180 days                                                  |
| Log files                                                                            | 30 days (`LOG_RETENTION_DAYS`)                                                                          |

| Open-tracking device data                                                            | Cleared 30 days after an open; none is recorded                                                         |

| A data-subject request                                                               | While open, however old; 1,095 days after it is closed                                                  |

## (g) Security measures

- **In code** (each step is listed with its commits at the end of `decisions_for_counsel.md`):
  - certificate and host-name checks on the inbox connection (G3);
  - erasure tokens keyed with `ERASURE_KEY`, which is kept outside the database, required at start-up and checked against the database's fingerprint of it (G7, G17);
  - erased values zeroed inside the database file, and its write-ahead log emptied after every erasure, retention run and journal replay (G8);
  - log files masked as they are written, one per command, kept 30 days (G9); keys, passwords, `Authorization` header values and IPv6 addresses are masked too;
  - AI minimisation: every AI switch off by default; every prompt stripped of addresses, phone numbers and link parameters; no call carrying a recipient's data while `AI_DPA_REFERENCE` is blank (G10);
  - data-subject requests recognised in replies, registered with their deadline, and worked with `main.py dsr` (G11);
  - retention, by the agent's daily run or by `main.py retention` without it (G12);
  - the source channel, basis and rules version on every contact, and a basis record after each live send (G13);
  - live sending refused without the legal identity, the privacy contact and the notice's address (G14), without an opt-out route, which is a public https unsubscribe address or inbox reading (G5d), and outside the enabled countries (G5e). With inbox reading on, the agent reads the Inbox and Junk Email folders of each sending mailbox, and live sending stops while an inbox has had no complete read within three check intervals, as when it cannot log in;
  - opt-outs, company blocks and data requests held on disk when the database is busy (G5b, G5c, G11);
  - the company-wide stop after an objection, the one-click button, a data request by reply or an erasure (G5c, G11);
  - the dashboard: it refuses to start without a `DASHBOARD_PASSWORD` of at least 12 characters, wherever it listens, this machine included, and never runs Flask's debugger; it shuts out for up to 15 minutes an address that types 10 wrong passwords, takes changes only from its own page, and runs its page under a strict Content-Security-Policy; it answers only to localhost, the `BASE_URL` host and the address it listens on, and keeps secret settings out of what it shows (G15, `docs/DEPLOY.md` section 3a);
  - the Docker image runs as an unprivileged user and holds neither the client list nor `data/` (G15); it also leaves out the documentation, tests and every spreadsheet, PDF and Markdown file, is built from an exact Python version, and runs read-only, published on this machine only, with no Linux capabilities; inside it, `data/`, its folders and the journal can be read by the app's own user only;
  - an `UNSUBSCRIBE_SECRET`, and a new database's `ERASURE_KEY`, of at least 32 characters;
  - backups checked before they count, the newest 14 kept; the journal; `main.py replay-journal` and `main.py restore-check` (G16); `main.py restore-drill`, run by IT every three months, which restores a backup in a temporary folder and fails if any opt-out, company block, address block, bounce, erasure, held opt-out or email sent would be lost or anyone erased would come back (security hardening, stages C and C2, and stage 2 of the second round, which also journals every email sent so that a restore sends none twice); a written incident plan, `docs/INCIDENT_PLAYBOOK.md`;
  - the agent and the dashboard refuse to start while the erasure key is shorter than 32 characters or is not the key the database was erased with (security hardening, stage C);
  - the preflight check (`main.py doctor`) warns when retention or a backup has not run for 2 days, a log file holds an address, token, key, password or IP in clear, an opt-out is held, the database's log has not been emptied, a request is overdue, or an AI switch that sees recipients' data is on while `AI_DPA_REFERENCE` is blank; an erasure key other than the database's fails it (G17);
  - unsubscribe links that carry a keyed code, not the address (G20);
  - Microsoft 365 mailboxes signed in with a certificate instead of a password, once IT sets up Microsoft sign-in: no mailbox password is kept on the machine, the app reaches only the mailboxes IT gives it, and the private key file is readable by the agent only and kept out of git and the Docker image (`docs/DEPLOY.md` section 12).
- **For IT** (decision I4, where the agent runs):
  - disk encryption on the host: the product encrypts none of the database, the backups, the journal, the access answers, the held opt-outs and the log files;
  - daily scheduled tasks for `main.py backup` and `main.py retention` (`docs/DEPLOY.md`, sections 8 and 9): nothing runs them on its own;
  - an encrypted copy of the backups off the machine, and a copy of the journal on another disk: a journal lost with the database cannot be replayed (L5.7);
  - who may log in to the machine: anyone who can, and who learns the dashboard password, can use the dashboard from there;
  - the administrators' access logs, if counsel finds that the Garante's measure on system administrators applies (L5.8).
